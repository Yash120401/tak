Buying Your First 30m Yacht: A Practical Checklist

From survey and sea trial to crew and running costs, the steps that matter before you sign.

By Yacht Affair Editorial
Share

A 30-metre yacht is a significant step up. At this size you are buying not just a boat but a small operation: a crew, a maintenance programme, berthing and insurance, all of which need planning before you make an offer. This checklist walks through the steps that matter most, in the order most buyers meet them.

1. Start with a clear brief

Before you browse, decide how you will use the yacht. Where will you cruise, and for how much of the year? How many guests will you usually host, and will they stay aboard overnight? Do you plan to offer her for charter? Your answers narrow the field quickly, from hull type and range to the number of cabins and crew you will need.

2. Shortlist and compare

Use the filters to compare yachts on length, year, builder, location and price, then save the listings that fit your brief. Specifications, photos, prices and availability come from the listing party and can change, so treat a listing as the start of a conversation rather than a final offer.

3. Talk to the listing party

When a yacht looks right, contact the broker or seller directly. Ask for the full specification, the maintenance and refit history, recent survey reports and the yacht’s documentation. A good broker will also tell you how long she has been on the market and why she is for sale.

Treat a listing as the start of a conversation, not a final offer. Verify everything before you commit.

4. Verify ownership and documentation

Confirm who owns the yacht and that the person selling it has the right to do so. Check the registration and flag, the builder’s certificate and bill-of-sale history, and whether there are any mortgages, liens or claims against the vessel. If you are dealing with a broker, check their identity and the licences they hold where they operate.

5. Commission a survey and sea trial

An independent marine surveyor, chosen by you, should inspect the hull, machinery and systems, ideally with the yacht out of the water. Follow the survey with a sea trial to see how she performs underway. Many purchase agreements make the sale conditional on both, which gives you room to renegotiate or withdraw if problems come to light.

6. Budget for running costs

Beyond the purchase price, plan for crew, berthing, insurance, fuel, routine maintenance, periodic refits and management. Running costs vary widely with how and where a yacht is used, so ask the broker and the current crew for real figures, and build in a contingency.

7. Protect your payments

Agree how the deposit and balance will be paid before you sign anything, and take independent legal advice on the purchase agreement. Be cautious of requests for wire transfers, deposits to unfamiliar accounts, or pressure to move the conversation away from where you found the listing. Yacht Affair does not hold funds or act as escrow; payments are made directly between the parties.

8. Plan the handover

Agree the closing date, the delivery location, the inventory that transfers with the yacht and whether the current crew will stay on. Arrange insurance from the moment ownership passes, and register the yacht under your chosen flag.

Your pre-purchase checklist

  • A written brief: cruising area, guests, crew and use
  • Full specification, history and documents from the listing party
  • Ownership, registration, liens and the seller’s authority verified
  • Independent survey and sea trial completed
  • A running-cost budget with a contingency
  • Payment terms agreed and legal advice taken
  • Insurance and registration ready for handover

This article is general information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Always take professional advice for your own circumstances.
