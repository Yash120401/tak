Buying Your First 30m Yacht: A Practical Checklist

From survey and sea trial to crew and running costs, the steps that matter before you sign.

By Yacht Affair Editorial · 09 Sep 2026

A 30-metre yacht is a significant step up. At this size you are buying not just a boat but a small operation: a crew, a maintenance programme, berthing and insurance, all of which need planning before you make an offer. This checklist walks through the steps that matter most, in the order most buyers meet them.

1. Start with a clear brief

Before you browse, decide how you will use the yacht. Where will you cruise, and for how much of the year? How many guests will you usually host, and will they stay aboard overnight? Your answers narrow the field quickly, from hull type and range to the number of cabins and crew you will need.

2. Shortlist and compare

Compare yachts on length, year, builder, location and price, then save the listings that fit your brief. Specifications, photos, prices and availability come from the listing brokerage and can change, so treat a listing as the start of a conversation rather than a final offer.

3. Ask for the documents

When a yacht looks right, ask the listing brokerage for the full specification, deck plans and brochure, then for the documents below. A good broker will also tell you how long she has been on the market and why she is for sale.

DocumentWhy it matters
Builder’s certificateConfirms where and when the yacht was built, and for whom
Bill-of-sale historyShows the chain of ownership up to the current seller
Registration certificateConfirms the flag and the current registered owner
Survey reportsRecord the yacht’s condition and any past issues
Maintenance and refit recordsShow how she has been looked after, and what is due next

4. Commission a survey and sea trial

An independent marine surveyor, chosen by you, should inspect the yacht, ideally out of the water. Follow the survey with a sea trial. Many purchase agreements make the sale conditional on both, which gives you room to renegotiate or withdraw if problems come to light.

5. Budget for running costs

Beyond the purchase price, plan for:

  • Crew salaries, training and rotation
  • Berthing, insurance and registration
  • Fuel, routine maintenance and periodic refits
  • Management, if you use a yacht management company

Running costs vary widely with how and where a yacht is used, so ask the broker and the current crew for real figures, and build in a contingency.

6. Protect your payments

Agree how the deposit and balance will be paid before you sign anything, and take independent legal advice on the purchase agreement. Be cautious of requests for wire transfers to unfamiliar accounts, or pressure to move quickly.

Your pre-purchase checklist

  • A written brief: cruising area, guests, crew and use
  • Full specification, deck plans and documents from the listing brokerage
  • Ownership, registration and liens verified
  • Independent survey and sea trial completed
  • A running-cost budget with a contingency
  • Payment terms agreed and legal advice taken
